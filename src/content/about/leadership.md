On most teams I end up holding the plan: scoping the problem, splitting the work, designing the system so everyone can build in parallel, and keeping it shipping when the clock runs out.

- **InnOlympics 2026 · Team lead** Led a 4-person team and owned the system design and backend orchestration for Nura, a multilingual healthcare navigator shipped in 24 hours.
- **Bantay-Dagitab · PM, architect and backend lead** Wrote the four JSON Schema contracts every module had to follow, so the team could build in parallel without breaking each other.
- **MERALCO IDOL 2025 · Team lead, Top 14 finalist** Led a 4-person team as system designer and PM; picked from a large field of applicants.
- **Gen AI Hackathon · Team lead, semi-finalist** Led team Joybread Studios on JuryBee, a multi-agent contract analyzer.
- **Trend Micro University Capture the Flag · 32nd of 200 teams** Competed as team JOYBREAD for NU Manila.
- **Also competed** Alibaba Hackathon, HACK4GOV, and DLSU Hacker Cup 2025.

My first hackathon taught me the most: I led a team without knowing what any of us were doing. Now I show up with a plan.
